**Pitch:** One tall tower, hard but fair, where a slip drops you back down; you climb it live while viewers race the same tower on their phones for your prize.

**Genre:** precision platformer, one long climb of thirty levels

**Controls:** A and D or the arrows to move, Space or W to jump (hold for a higher jump), F to fire the blaster in the worlds that give one, hold R to start the climb again, Esc for the menu, M to mute, C on the title to check a viewer's code, 1 2 3 or the arrows on the title to pick the difficulty, B to continue. In a race: left click or E to shove the climber beside you (or the one hanging on your feet), right click or Q to catch hold of a climber's feet and be carried; Space leaps off them, Q lets go; left click also fires the blaster. While you watch a race: the arrows change climber. On the room screen: the arrows change the seats and the finish level, G opens the level picker, 1 2 3 the difficulty, Tab picks a player, V moves them between playing and watching, K kicks them (the host only), C copies the link, Enter starts. A controller (PS5, Xbox): stick or d-pad to move, Cross/A jump, Circle/B or R1/RB shove, Triangle/Y or L1/LB hang on, Square/X or R2/RT fire, Options/Menu for the menu. The main menu is worked with the arrows and Enter, or by tapping. On a phone: two arrow buttons on the left, a jump button on the right, a fire button where there is a blaster, and shove and hang-on buttons in a race; every button of the menus and the room screen is tapped. *(guessed)*

**Question:** Is one long, punishing climb fun to play and to watch for an hour? *(guessed)*

**Hooks:** A fall is never a reset: you drop until something below catches you, so every metre is at risk and viewers watch the height bar. Finishing gives a code with your time, so a viewer can prove they beat you.

**Intent:** soft or no failure *(provisional)*

## First minute
You pick a difficulty and stand in a garden at the foot of the tower with the height bar at zero. There is no warm-up: the first ledges are already a real jump apart, and you learn tap for a hop, hold for a full jump, by missing. You miss one, fall, and land a few ledges down instead of dying. A gold star hangs a full jump above a late ledge. At about 30 metres you land on the first gold floor: a chime plays, a banner shows level 1 of 30 with how long it took, and the climb is kept from there.

## Challenge
*(provisional)* Thirty levels in ten worlds of three, about 900 metres, with no easy levels: level 1 starts a third of the way up the difficulty range (`start_hard` in config.js), and each level is narrower and wider-gapped than the last until level 30 sits at the limit of the jump. A new world every three levels keeps something new coming. The worlds add, in order: moving ledges, crumbling ledges, buttons that open a gate and timed ledges, ice, sun and moon (every jump swaps which ledges are solid; the wind blows there too on Hard only), a blaster with targets to shoot and red bumpers, bouncers and conveyor belts, turrets, and a last ultimate world with everything at once. Machines and the blaster come only in the worlds that need them. Nothing kills you; a miss costs height. Three difficulties: easy has wider ledges, normal has a gold floor after every level, hard has narrower ledges and a floor only every third level, so a miss can cost a whole world. How long a first clear takes is not known yet.

## Core loop
- Read the next jump and commit to it.
- Land it and gain height, or miss and fall to whatever is below.
- Reach a gold rest floor: a chime, a banner, a safe place to breathe.
- Reach the top: your time, your falls and a code to share.

## Goal & fail state
- Reach the top of the tower; the ending shows your time, your falls and your finish code. *(provisional)*
- No death and no game over: a fall costs height. R starts the climb again. *(provisional)*
